# OVERCLOCK

### You have no health. You have a clock. It only falls. Killing is the only thing that winds it back up.

A pixelated first-person shooter where **time is your health bar**. Runs in a browser.

## ▶ [**CLICK HERE TO PLAY**](https://patheticpants.github.io/AndersTestGame/)

No download, no install, no account. It runs in your browser.

*(Or grab the code and open `index.html` by double-clicking it — no build step,
no server, no dependencies.)*

---

## The point of it

There are no assets in this repository. Not one `.png`, `.wav`, `.mp3` or `.ttf`.

Every wall texture, every enemy, every weapon, every gunshot and every note of
the soundtrack is **generated by code at load time**, and the 3D is drawn by a
software renderer written from scratch — the same ray casting technique id
Software used in 1993, implemented per-pixel in JavaScript.

The loading bar on startup is real work: it is building the palette, painting
the textures and sculpting the sprites.

## The hook

Most shooters give you a health bar and scatter medkits around the floor, which
quietly teaches you to play slowly: back off, heal up, peek a corner. This one
deletes the health bar and replaces it with **a countdown**.

The clock starts around 100 seconds and falls the whole time you are alive.
Nothing you do stops it. What you *can* do is wind it back up:

| | |
|---|---|
| land damage | **+0.03s per point** — a continuous drip, so a long fight pays you the whole way through |
| kill | **+3s** |
| execute a staggered enemy | **+9s** |
| time cells and orbs | **+3s to +18s** |
| keycards and secrets | **+8s / +12s** |

And every one of those is multiplied by your **kill chain**, up to 2x.

The result is a shooter where retreating is a losing move and hesitation is
literally fatal — but where a good fight leaves you *better off than you
started*. Watching the number climb back up while a room burns is the entire
game.

## The three tools

**CHRONO SURGE** (`Shift`) — the world drops to a quarter speed while you keep
most of yours. Rockets hang in the air, fireballs crawl past your head. It burns
your clock at triple rate, so it is the most expensive button in the game and
the one that saves your life.

**DASH** (`Space`) — three charges, brief invulnerability, and a lens kick.
Dashing *through* an attack in chrono is the best feeling here.

**STAGGER AND EXECUTE** (`F`) — the first lethal hit does not kill. It drops the
enemy to a knee, strobing, for four seconds. Close in and execute: the screen
freezes for a beat, it bursts, and time orbs drift toward you. **The seconds
keeping you alive are inside the thing trying to kill you.**

Every weapon also has a **secondary on right mouse**: a piercing railshot, a
double blast that knocks *you* backwards hard enough to use as movement, a flak
burst, a slow shock orb, and a cluster rocket that airbursts into bomblets.

Sector one teaches all of this in-flow — one prompt at a time, each appearing
the moment it becomes relevant and disappearing the instant you use it. There is
nothing to read before you start.

## What's in the engine

**Rendering** — `src/renderer.js`
- Per-column DDA ray casting against a tile grid, with textured wall columns
- Per-row textured floor and ceiling casting, plus angle-mapped sky tiles
- Thin sliding doors resolved *inside* the DDA loop, so a half-open door
  correctly shows the gap and lets rays through it
- Billboard sprites, depth sorted and clipped against a per-column z-buffer
- 32-level light diminishing via a precomputed colormap, so shading a pixel is
  one array lookup — this is why 320×200 with full floor casting runs in about a
  millisecond per frame
- Emissive masks: computer screens, light strips and lava stay bright in the dark
- Muzzle flashes light the actual geometry; dash and chrono widen the lens

**Art** — `src/palette.js`, `src/pixbuf.js`, `src/textures.js`, `src/sprites.js`
- A 256-colour palette built from tuned ramps, and a 32×256 colormap computed by
  nearest-colour search
- An indexed-bitmap drawing DSL (shaded ellipses, capsule limbs, tapered tubes,
  scanline polygons)
- First-person weapons built as foreshortened tubes with the hands running off
  the bottom edge — they sit below the sightline and stop short of the horizon,
  which is what makes a gun read as *held* rather than as scenery
- Enemies drawn in three views and mirrored at render time, giving eight
  rotations — they visibly turn as they circle you
- Two skies: a freezing star-field with an aurora for the surface sectors, and
  hellfire for the Core

**Sound** — `src/audio.js`
- Every effect synthesised from oscillators, filtered noise and wave shapers
- Distance attenuation and stereo panning relative to where you're facing
- A step sequencer with a different riff per sector, which drops into a drag
  when chrono engages

**Game** — `src/entities.js`, `src/game.js`, `src/levels.js`
- The clock economy, tuned against a bot: damage-per-second income has to be
  able to outrun the drain, a single hit is capped so nothing one-shots a run,
  brief i-frames stop swarms chain-melting you, and below 14 seconds every kill
  sheds bonus orbs so a bad fight is recoverable instead of terminal
- Two clocks: the world runs on scaled time, you run on your own, and a brief
  hit stop freezes both so executions land with weight
- Monster state machine (idle → chase → attack → pain → **stagger** → die) with
  Doom-style direction picking, wall sliding and soft body separation
- **Infighting**: a monster hit by another monster's shot turns on it
- Hitscan, piercing and projectile weapons, splash damage, cluster bomblets,
  chain-reacting barrels
- Keycards, secret doors, damaging slime and lava, auto-closing doors that
  monsters can shove open
- Three hand-authored maps, validated at load

**HUD** — `src/hud.js`
- The countdown sits dead centre bottom, because it is the only number that
  matters; the screen itself reddens and pulses under 15 seconds and the
  soundtrack tightens with it
- Corner readouts over a full-screen view: shield, dash charges, weapon and
  reserve, kill-chain rank
- A bitmap font drawn from bit patterns, and a revealed-as-you-explore automap

## Layout

```
index.html          loader + shell
src/palette.js      palette and light colormaps
src/pixbuf.js       indexed bitmap + drawing primitives
src/textures.js     walls, floors, ceilings, skies
src/sprites.js      enemies, weapons, items, effects
src/audio.js        sound synthesis and the music sequencer
src/levels.js       map data
src/entities.js     enemies, items, projectiles, orbs, AI
src/renderer.js     the software 3D renderer
src/hud.js          heads-up display, automap, menus
src/game.js         world building, player, chrono, weapons, main loop
```

The 320×200 framebuffer is presented at a 4:3 display ratio on purpose. Those
non-square pixels are what the game is supposed to look like.
